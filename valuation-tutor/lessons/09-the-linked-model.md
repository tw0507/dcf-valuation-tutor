# Lesson 9: Balancing the model

**Time:** about 70 min · **Needs:** lesson 8
**Goal:** the learner assembles assumptions, schedules and all three statements into one linked three-year model that balances in every year, and can debug one that doesn't.

This is the payoff of lessons 5 to 8: every earlier schedule (drivers, working capital, fixed assets, debt) plugs in here. Tabs: Worked Example, Practice 1, Practice 2.

---

## 1. Story (no jargon)

Maya has four separate pieces of paper: her sales plan, her customer-payment plan, her truck plan and her loan plan. The bank wants one thing: a plan where they all agree with each other.

So she puts them on one page, in a set order. The sales plan sets revenue and costs. The truck plan adds the wear-and-tear cost. The loan plan sets the interest. That gives her profit. Then the cash trace starts from that profit, adds back the wear and tear, takes off the cash stuck with customers, the new trucks, the loan repayments and the dividends she pays herself. The cash trace tells her the cash at the end of each year.

Finally, the snapshot: cash from the trace, customer money and ingredient stock from the payment plan, trucks from the truck plan, loans from the loan plan, and kept profits growing by profit minus dividends. If every piece is connected properly, the snapshot balances in every year. If it doesn't, something is double-counted or missing, and the size of the gap is the clue.

## 2. Reveal

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Integrated" / "fully linked" model | every number flows from assumptions through schedules to the statements |
| "Supporting schedules" | the working capital, PP&E and debt blocks feeding the statements |
| "Order of build" | assumptions → income statement → schedules → cash flow → balance sheet → check |
| "Dividend payout ratio" | dividends ÷ net income (30%) |
| "Balance check" | must be 0 in every year; `ROUND(...,2)` avoids tiny decimal noise |

Debugging rules to state:
1. **Look at the size of the gap.** Does it equal a line item, or twice one (a sign error)?
2. **Check the first year that breaks.** Fix that year first.
3. **Check each link in the list** (net income, D&A, working capital, capex, debt, dividends, cash).
4. **Never plug.** A plug hides the bug in every scenario you'll ever run.
